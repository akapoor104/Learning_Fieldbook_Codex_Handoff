# Learning Fieldbook — Design specification v0.8

**Design edition v0.8 · November 22, 2026**

A family journal, a planner for projects that cross subjects, and a portfolio of what Anush has already done. See the companion **Learning_Fieldbook_Visual_Walkthrough_v0.8.pdf** (20 pages).

**For Anush and his family.** At his next turn on the family computer, Anush signs in and types one sentence about math club. If a parent took a picture, they can add it from their own account before or after he writes. On Friday he can revisit the moment and choose one thing to try. Years later, he can still open the original work, read his own explanation, and decide what someone else may see.

**For the builder.** Use Rust with Axum for the backend, a REST-style JSON API, PostgreSQL 16 and a Svelte/TypeScript frontend. Svelte Flow draws the project graph. Home, Render and Google Cloud use the same release. Sections 4 and 5 follow one club experience into a project, an essay plan and an achievement. Section 6 defines the interfaces, §7 the hosting profiles, §8 the build tickets and §9 the restore procedure. In the companion walkthrough, page 9 shows the project graph, page 11 the Google Docs writing flow, page 12 the outcome, page 13 the portfolio preview, page 15 the architecture, page 17 project recovery page 18 the paper worksheet, page 19 cumulative prototypes and page 20 the Failure Résumé. The walkthrough uses fictional examples. All build and performance checks below are acceptance criteria for the proposed application.

## 1. The agreed family experience

| Interview choice | First-release behavior |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Family journal and private diary | New ordinary moments go to the family journal. Anush can choose his private diary at capture. Household members comment under their own names. Anush owns his writing. |
| 2. Two entrances to Explore | Available when Anush taps Explore and when he chooses a moment during the weekly check-in. Keeping a memory is a complete action. |
| 3. Selected entries for relatives | An invited relative can read and comment on the specific entry or highlight shared with them. The family chooses any accompanying files. |
| 4. Either parent can recover | Anush normally reads his diary alone. Either Anand or Rachana can independently open or recover it when necessary. Anush can see which parent used this access and when. The rule follows restored copies. |
| 5. Originals and longer recordings | Archive photos, original PDFs, project files, and short demos. Keep full-length recordings as Drive links by default. **Keep a copy** archives a selected file. |
| 6. One suggested connection | Offer at most one earlier family-journal moment, explain why it may fit, and let Anush accept or dismiss it. |
| 7. One experiment | Offer one small, specific experiment per request. Anush can try it, save it for later, dismiss it, or return for another idea. |
| 8. Family enters outside feedback | The family uploads the teacher’s letter or records a dated quotation or paraphrase. Teacher and mentor evidence carries the writer’s and recorder’s roles. Direct commenting belongs to household members and explicitly invited relatives. |
| 9. Local guidance with optional outside AI | The built-in guide works inside Fieldbook. A guardian may enable an eligible service. Anush selects and previews the exact excerpt each time and chooses whether to send it. |
| 10. Home pilot with ready hosting choices | Pilot at home using PostgreSQL and a private file store. Supply repeatable Render and Google Cloud profiles, cost inputs, a migration rehearsal, and rollback instructions as part of the initial build. |
| 11. Capture depends on who is present | When Anush is alone, open the sentence editor on a shared computer. With a parent, offer writing and adding a photo side by side. A parent can contribute later from their own account. |
| 12. Start a project and shape its first idea | Create a first pretotype draft when the project begins. **Write first idea** and **Surprise me** are equal ways to develop the plan in its map. Returning to the map restores the selected idea. |
| 13. Plan in Fieldbook, draft in Google Docs (B) | Keep the outline, reader order, sources, paragraph jobs and revision notes in Fieldbook. Anush writes the essay in his chosen restricted Google Doc. He can save a dated PDF of a milestone. |

| Family goal | Product decision | How the family can tell it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rigor | For a project he cares about, Anush can name a question, what would convince him, his attempt, the help he used, and a revision. | He can point to a difference between two attempts and explain what the evidence supports. |
| Visibility | A dated moment can appear under a project and several skills. Source, student voice, and feedback stay distinguishable. | A parent can find an example of explaining an idea in both math club and another setting. |
| Recognition | Anush chooses evidence for a one-page **Independent Learning Record** or **Mentor Brief**. Both identify the family as the source. | A reader can tell what Anush did, who observed it, and what is still in progress. |

**What connects the three activities.** Anush can leave a journal memory as it is, use it to start a project, or select it for his portfolio. An old Scout outing or club achievement is as welcome as something he records today. He can also learn Rust and frontend development by building a small part of this application on invented data.

**First field trial.** Start with math club and one interest Anush chooses. Offer the Friday check-in within the family's existing routine. After two weeks, ask which entries he wanted to keep, which field felt like homework, and whether he could find one example without adult help. Treat “I’d leave that out” as useful feedback. Casual play can stay outside the portfolio.

| S21 | A proud moment predates the app | A parent records an earlier tournament, its activity date, Anush’s role and the reported result. Anush adds his own recollection. | S22 | Music, math and coding share one project | Open Graph from a fraction lesson, split the game into branches, and connect the rhythm maker’s output to the display’s input. | The same selected idea, outgoing agreement and incoming evidence are available in Graph and List. | S23 | Today’s experiment becomes a new achievement | After an actual trial, Anush records what happened and chooses Add to portfolio. He may add another project or skill the observation supports. | One dated source remains attached to the chosen highlight. A proposed idea, a sample run or a checked box alone produces zero portfolio items. |
| S24 | Anush will be away from the computer | Print an ordered checklist for a project or one branch. Cross off steps on paper, add a thought and later enter the marks he wants to keep. | The same item code identifies each step after a rename or reorder. Saving twice creates no new work or evidence. |
| S25 | Several small tests guide two builds | Run three pretotypes, then select P1 and P2 for one version and P2 and P3 for another. Write what each version does with the evidence. | Open P2 from either version and see one unchanged observation and both destination builds. |
| S26 | A failed attempt becomes a point of pride | Feature an actual missed prediction and an unexpected build failure, print the project’s Failure Résumé, then revive one idea under a changed condition. | The first result, learner’s words and original date remain visible beside the linked new attempt. |

**A worked path from one moment to two uses.** In a sample math-club lesson, two paper strips begin at different places. Anush notices that comparing their right edges gives a misleading answer. He aligns the starting points and marks the equal lengths. His assistant hands out the strips. On Friday he writes, “Next time I’ll ask someone to explain why the starting points matter.” His term record can use the before-and-after pair to show mathematical explanation. A mentor brief can use that same pair to ask how he might tell whether a learner understands. He can keep his feelings about leading the session in his diary.

**How ownership can grow.** In grades 5–6, let Anush choose one moment while an adult can scribe or organize files. In middle school, he can set his own success criterion, compare attempts, and invite feedback. In high school, he can curate original files and explain the limits of an individual contribution on a team. These are optional uses of the same records. A birthday or grade change never advances a skill or brings a longer form onto Home.

**Navigation.** Home, Projects, Skills and Portfolio are the four persistent destinations. Portfolio contains Achievements and Keepsakes. Existing Keepsakes links open that tab. Put capture within one tap of Home and available from a project with that project preselected. Put family settings under the signed-in account. Use working links for Back, Cancel, and Edit in forms. Return to the saved item after a successful write.

**Visual direction.** Make the app feel like a well-kept family notebook. Use a warm paper background `#FBF7EE`, dark ink `#203B34`, a deep evergreen primary action `#185646` with white text, and soft sage `#E3EFE5`, apricot `#FFE1C1`, and lilac `#EEE6F5` as card backgrounds. Put dark text on the pale fills. Use a readable serif for project titles (Baskervville or Georgia) and a simple sans serif for forms (DM Sans or system sans). Keep original work large enough to inspect. A fraction-strip sketch can be a project thumbnail. Give empty projects a simple labeled placeholder.

**For a shared computer and a parent’s Android device.** Use one primary button per step, visible field labels, everyday verbs, 16 px or larger body type, and at least 44 × 44 CSS px action targets. Check 360 px and a laptop-width layout, keyboard-only navigation, visible focus, inline errors, and 200% zoom. Provide text for meaningful images and a caption alternative for links. Preserve inputs on a validation error. Keep optional detail folded. Show counts when they help users find search results. Use a 4.5:1 minimum contrast target for normal text. [W3C contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)

## 4. Evidence, voice, and family permissions

**The smallest useful record.** One sentence or a photo is enough. Optionally attach several files, links, an outcome, who helped, or what sparked an aha moment. A user can correct the activity date or file a loose moment later. A family memory can end here.

**Asynchronous family capture.** On Anush’s shared-computer account, give keyboard focus to **Write a sentence** when he chooses to capture. Show **Add a photo** as a secondary action. On a parent’s own account, **Working together** offers writing and photo upload side by side. The signed-in actor is always visible. On a shared screen a parent’s typed recollection is a guardian observation. If a parent scribes what Anush says, use the existing confirmation flow. The parent may attach an original directly to an accessible family entry or place it in **Family photos to match** with learner, date and optional project. Anush can link an unmatched photo later or leave it there. Link by asset ID, preserve uploader and original bytes, and apply separate inclusion and sharing permissions. A proposed match never changes an entry silently. The parent workflow cannot attach a file to the child’s diary. On a day without computer time, he can jot a line on paper. Later, he can enter it under its activity date, or a parent can scribe it with the existing attribution rule. A solo sentence needs only his account and a browser.

**Signing in without a phone.** Support an individual device credential in Anush’s own OS account or a user-verifying security key in a compatible computer browser. A guardian’s credential stays with the guardian. Give shared-device users visible **Sign out** and **Switch account** actions. End the prior server session, clear account-specific in-memory state and sensitive drafts, and recheck authorization on browser-back navigation. Choose a shared-device idle timeout and warn the writer before expiry. Keep a validated learner-only draft on the server if the learner saves one. Avoid storing diary content in shared browser storage. Rehearse the actual device and both parent recovery paths before depending on them. [Web Authentication on computers](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Authentication_API)

**Skills.** Start with Explain an idea, Test and improve, Solve with evidence, Work with others, Lead and serve, and Organize my work. The family can edit these labels. A skill link carries a sentence explaining the evidence. Optional self-descriptions are “With help” and “On my own.” A claim of using a skill somewhere new links two entries from different projects and explains the connection. Show dated examples and who observed them. School credits and Scout advancement are recorded by their respective issuers.

**Sources of voice.** A student writes and edits their own moment. A guardian can add a separate moment or observation as themselves. If an adult scribes a child’s spoken words, label them “Anush’s words, entered by parent” and let Anush confirm before those words appear in an outward-facing edition. Label a family member’s recollection as a paraphrase. A teacher’s words entered by a parent remain attributed to both speaker and recorder. Keep quotes exact. Feedback can be accepted, questioned, or left unused by the student.

**Help and authorship.** A moment can identify solo or team work, the piece Anush did, sources he adapted, help from a person or AI, and how he checked the result. He might write, “A tool suggested two ways to test the turn. I picked one, ran both settings, and compared the end positions.” Each selected item in an external page requires a short **My part** line. Label learner reflection, observation, and family summary separately. Explore can suggest one small experiment, and original student explanations remain identifiable. Preserve the original words, leave skill judgments to people, and take witness statements only from their attributed source.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Anush | Reads and edits his journal and diary, replies to dated comments, selects related work and evidence. | Can copy selected words to a new family highlight. The diary original stays in his diary. |
| Each parent, under a separate login | Reads the family journal, writes comments, adds attributed feedback or documents, manages media and recovery. | Either parent may independently open the private diary when needed through **Parent access**. The screen explains the access record before opening. |
| Named relative | A personal invitation grants access to one selected family entry or highlight, its selected attachments, and the relative’s conversation with the household. | May comment as themselves. The family can revoke the grant. Later entries, attachments, household-only comments, and other relatives’ threads require separate selection. |
| Teacher, mentor, coach, or friend | Gives a letter or feedback to the family through their usual channel. | A household member records who wrote or said it, who entered it, the date, and any permission to reuse it. |
| Recipient of a printable page | Reads the approved edition the family gives them. | A dated snapshot has its own items and permissions. A saved PDF remains with its recipient. |

### Diary and guest sharing

On Anush’s diary screen write, **“This diary is yours. If Mom or Dad needs to open it, you can see who opened it and when.”** In ordinary household use, family feeds, counts, searches, project lists, thumbnails, Explore matches, relative pages, and shared check-ins draw only on family-visible records. Enforce this on the server for individual records and files. A private entry can be a complete thought with no project or learning purpose.

Either parent can reauthenticate with their own account and start a time-limited diary access session for this learner. Record parent ID, time, whether it is viewing or recovery, and session expiry before revealing content. Show the event in Anush’s access history on his next sign-in. End the elevated session on sign-out or expiry. Keep its routes read-only and private-cache controlled. The family backup carries encrypted diary content. Restoration requires the same role checks and a fresh access event to read it through Fieldbook. This access history covers use through the app. People entrusted with the host and disaster-recovery keys are trusted operators.

For a relative, show the student and guardian a guest preview of the chosen entry or exact highlight and explicitly chosen files. Record the named relative, selected material and current version. A single-use invitation binds a separately authenticated guest account to that grant. Adding a file, importing a letter, or editing an entry leaves the previous guest selection intact until the family updates it. Check the grant on every read, comment, and download. Revocation stops future access through the app. A family may manually give an invitation to a relative without email integration.

### What becomes part of the archive

| When a family member selects this | Default | What they can see years later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Achievement photo, drawing, certificate, or teacher’s PDF | Keep the original file bytes in private storage. Generate separate, metadata-stripped display images where helpful. | Original, activity date, uploader, creator or observer, description, and any approved audience. A mentor’s signature or PDF page stays intact. |
| Scratch game, code, CAD/3D design, or AI-assisted invention | Keep the selected source/project file and a short demo. A repository or playable URL can sit beside them. | The saved version, the child’s individual work, tools or collaborators, and how the child tested it. Source archives are delivered as downloads. |
| Short video or audio demo | Keep the selected original. The user marks it as a demo. | Original and caption, such as what to watch or listen for. |
| Full performance or tournament recording | Save the Drive or other HTTPS link and a human-readable label. **Keep a copy** lets the user select the original file. | “Drive link” and “Copy saved” are different states. Use the latter only after byte length and checksum verification. |
| Recommendation intended for direct submission | Track the request/status if the family does not possess a shareable copy. | The intended recipient and status. The family can keep any separately provided letter for its permitted purpose. |

Every moment accepts several ordered attachments. Display the audience per attachment. A private PDF alongside a shared photo stays private. Record `written_by` independently from `added_by`. A scanned letter reads “Written by teacher · uploaded by parent.” A conversation reads “Teacher’s observation · paraphrased by parent.” A guest sees only the approved representation. Keep originals, captions, and display renditions distinct.

**Android and Google.** Offer **Choose photos**, **Choose a file**, and **Paste a Drive link**. The Android photo picker can expose selected Google Photos on supported devices. A parent can scan a letter with the Google Drive Android app, then select the resulting PDF through Files/Drive or upload a downloaded copy. These are user-initiated imports. A locally held archival copy remains available when an outside URL or permission changes. Offer the Android picker in the parent flow. The learner’s independent login works on a shared computer with his own credential and a household recovery method. Google advises that primarily child-directed apps should avoid Google Sign-In and APIs that access Google Account data. The service can run on Google Cloud independently of the learner’s Google identity. ### How Explore helps

1. Anush opens one moment or selects a family moment during the check-in. Offer **Just keep this** alongside **Explore**. A person using the parent diary-access screen has no coaching or sharing action.
2. Use a small versioned collection of prompts keyed to activity or skill. Inside Fieldbook, look for one earlier, accessible family entry in another project with a shared skill. Sort by the number of shared skills, then latest activity date, then stable ID. Exclude the current entry, previously dismissed pair, private diary, and previous entries the learner cannot see. Offer a connection only when a real signal exists. Explain the signal in ordinary language. **Use this connection** and **Skip** both lead to a complete experience.
3. Give specific encouragement anchored to the selected work and **one experiment** with an observable result. For a game where players guess, “In the next round, ask one player to build one half from quarter-pieces before they score. What do they use to decide?” Save the outcome only if Anush wants to. Any later experiment requires another request. An experiment is a suggestion, and his eventual account of what happened is student-authored.
4. An optional **A thought from history** opens an exact, curated quotation with author, work, source, and why it fits. For a transfer between robotics and math club, George Pólya asks, “Can you use the result, or the method, for some other problem?” Keep historical quotations in a human-verified catalog. Omit the card when there is no good match. [Pólya, *How to Solve It*, looking back](https://archive.math.arizona.edu/lkondek/302/handouts/Polya.htm)
5. The optional **Outside wording help** control appears only if a guardian has enabled a service eligible for the learner’s age, data, and use. Anush sees the provider name, the exact editable excerpt, and **Send these words**. Record provider/config version, consent event, time, and a receipt without recording the raw excerpt in logs. The only personal content in the provider request is the previewed text. The template instruction is fixed and inspectable. Attachments, identities, family comments, the candidate related entry, and the rest of the journal are never appended automatically. A private entry can supply words only when Anush himself selects them in the private view. The saved response inherits that view’s visibility.
6. A canceled preview makes zero calls. On an unavailable provider, unsuitable terms, timeout, or unusable response, show the built-in suggestion. Keep any provider response labeled as a suggestion. In tests a fake provider records the outbound fields. A first release may ship with local guidance and a tested, disabled external connector until the operator qualifies a service. Google’s Gemini Developer API terms exclude apps likely to be accessed by people under 18. OpenAI’s under-18 API guidance requires implementing zero data retention before processing under-13 personal data. A provider and any required controls must be confirmed for this deployment before activation. [Gemini Developer API age requirements](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) · [OpenAI under-18 API guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/safety-checks/under-18-api-guidance)

### The Idea map

Anush can open **Idea map** from a family moment or project. The map helps plan the first pretotype or a later build. It starts with his question and the two equally available choices **Write first idea** and **Surprise me**. The existing **Explore** action still offers one small experiment. The map is a place to keep branches across visits. Anush can type an idea of his own even when the suggestion catalog has no match.

| What Anush wants | What he can do | What Fieldbook keeps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A fresh angle | Choose **Borrow from another field**. Ask for a surprise or name a field. A single draft shows the borrowed method, why it might fit, where it might fail and one way to test it. | He may edit, keep, skip or park it. An accepted card retains its source and his edits. A skipped draft does not enter the map. |
| To follow a hunch | Select a saved idea and branch again, or write **My own idea**. Pick two saved cards and choose **Combine these ideas**. | A new idea points to both originals with a short explanation. Each original remains available for another branch. The child can move between the branches he saved. |
| To figure out where to start | Choose **Make it smaller**. The app offers up to three short, editable steps for the *one* experiment he chose, with a visible sign of completion. | The next doable step is highlighted. For a full neighborhood, include an accessible display label and source on every node, the saved order on each `contains` edge and an accessible label on other relations. Index parent/position, both edge directions and project–moment links with household/learner scope. Derive containment from one parent record to prevent two conflicting sources of truth. Document `inspired_by`, `combined_into` and `feedback_next` separately. Duplicate edges or hidden source titles never enter the payload.

**Graph query rules.** Scope every query to household, learner, map and underlying object visibility. An edge is visible only when its two endpoints are accessible. Apply the rule before computing lists, degrees, continuation counts or search matches. The same endpoint feeds Map and List. A source later made private disappears from family traversal and search. Keep derived family material hidden pending explicit review if it quotes the restricted source. Scope accepted proposal uniqueness to map, focus and card revision. Store a combination's two incoming edges atomically. Reject self-links, duplicate edges, and ancestry cycles for derived nodes. Use revisions to reject concurrent edits with a recoverable conflict. Include graph source labels, dispositions and steps in the portable archive.

**Planning invariants.** Keep structural containment, creative provenance and output-to-input flow as distinct relations. A move or a sibling reorder touches outline order only. A split preserves its parent’s existing contracts and references. Serialize structural or dependency edits per map in one transaction, check the expected revision, and reject a move under oneself or a descendant. An add, split or connect uses an actor-scoped idempotency key and request digest. Identical retries return the first result. Different content under the same key is a conflict. Return the latest outline on a stale revision with the submitted text available for retry.

For required same-pass dependencies, validate the proposed producer → consumer link against the committed graph and preserve a feasible topological order. A same-pass path back to the producer is a conflict with a readable explanation. Optional references and next-revision feedback have their own labels and do not block rehearsal. External starting inputs (a teacher prompt, an actual measurement, a verified source) can have a named source and evidence instead of a producer node. Never infer dependencies from siblings' positions, dates, or inspiration edges. Browsing uses a paged neighborhood. Cycle checks and integration readiness use the complete authorized map within its household, not the visible page.

**What a passing check means.** Mark a rehearsal only as **Sample passed**. An actual pass pins the producer result, consumer revision, agreed contract and criteria. If any checked input, output, contract or consumer behavior changes, mark its affected real checks **Needs review** and retain their history. Follow downstream impact where a later result explicitly uses the changed artifact. An unchanged independent branch keeps its status. Show an open input, missing format/unit, revision mismatch or unmet criterion separately. Structural validation uses built-in safe checks on data. A semantic decision about prose, physical work or learning requires an identified person’s review. Uploaded program files remain passive attachments. Reading position changes in an essay flag old/new adjacent transitions for review while preserving the handoff topology.

**Planning privacy.** Authorize every node, endpoint, sample, artifact, comment, contract history and downstream impact query within the same household and learner map. Apply access before returning counts or breadcrumbs. If a source becomes restricted, withdraw any revealing descendants, contents or results from ordinary views pending review. Cross-family or diary-to-map links are refused. Relative previews and frozen keepsakes contain only their explicitly selected fields. Planning examples and drafts leave the household only through the existing audience and exact-excerpt consent controls.

**Upload and storage failure behavior.** The first save works with text alone, one picture, or several attachments. Default to 8 MiB upload parts for large files so each HTTP/1 request fits the Cloud Run limit. Keep upload IDs/offsets in PostgreSQL and chunks in the configured private store. A request may resume on another instance. Treat retries of identical parts as the same write. Validate the complete byte count and digest, then commit the ready asset and relation once. Expire abandoned staging objects. A failed transfer keeps the note and the option to retry or save the Drive link. Keep the actual original bytes and serve sanitized previews when sharing. Validate supported formats, bound decoding, and treat PDFs, zipped project files and source code as passive files. Store cloud links as user-supplied pointers without fetching arbitrary URLs on the server. [Cloud Run size limits](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/quotas)
